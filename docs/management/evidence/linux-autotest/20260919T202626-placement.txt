NOT proof of absence, and weaker than it looks. Two separate reasons:
  1. ILLUSTRATIVE ARITHMETIC ONLY, not a confidence estimate: if the per-sample
     rate were the overnight runs' 3-in-9 and the samples were independent and
     comparable, seeing nothing in 3 would have probability (2/3)^3 = 0.30. None
     of those assumptions holds here - the rate is not known, and reason 2 says
     the samples are not comparable, which makes the figure self-undermining as
     a measure of anything. It is kept only to show that a clean run of this
     size would be unsurprising even if the fault were common.
  2. The SAMPLES ARE NOT COMPARABLE, and this is the real point. The 3-in-9 came from long
     campaign runs: several cases at once, clues sitting unfound for in-game hours,
     stale-clue relocation (RELOCATE_AFTER_HOURS=72) running, and saves and reloads
     between cases. This check verifies ONE case per fresh world immediately after
     placement, with no time passing and no relocation. It may simply not create
     the conditions the fault needs.
