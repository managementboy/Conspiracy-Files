WHY

  questions, and warns that metadata checks cannot establish coherent or funny
  writing. I had changed 30 finding sentences and added 14 objects and 5
  carriers, so the obligation was mine twice over: the measurements say the
  relationships are distributed, they cannot say the sentences read.

WHAT WAS READ
  Whole cases rendered as a player sees them - every record in discovery
  order with the comparisons the organiser would show - via the projection,
  not the stored case.

CASE: "Why were the winners' bats paid for before the prize order existed?"
  Three records: a paid bat order, a committee note, a collection sheet.
  Reads correctly. Each source adds a fact; the survivor observes without
  concluding; the institutional joke lands and pays off twice.

  My reclassified comparison, shown under "Does not match":
    "The receipt reads as a prize bought for a winner. The committee
     instruction has twelve identical bats bought for everybody who entered."
  Heading and sentence now agree, which was the point of rewriting them: the
  old text ("The committee instruction explains both the early payment and the
  WINNER imprint") read as explanation under a heading that says conflict.

  The object closes it:
    "Baseball bat, marked Ines Kubiak - A bat standing behind the prize
     paperwork, WINNER stencilled along it. A name is inked on the knob:
     Ines Kubiak. One of the twelve, kept back. Every one of them says WINNER."
  No headings, which is right - nothing is written on a bat. It carries the
  name and claims nothing more. The last line does the work.

CASE: "Why was final pay prepared before the cook resigned?"
  The cook refused to count stock unpaid and left; the motel filed it as
  voluntary. The pile is the evidence:
    "Nine tins of soup - Nine of them on the shelf, turned label-out. A pencil
     tick on each lid. Somebody counted these and marked every one. The
     inventory was never finished."
  That is the object belonging to its event rather than being attached to a
  passing case, which is what the owner's decision asked for. A generic pile
  of tins would have said nothing here; these are the tins the story is about.

AGAINST THE FOUR QUESTIONS
  What happened, and what does each source add?   Clear in both.
  What can be inferred, what remains open?        Both cases leave the actual
    outcome outside the file and say so, without pretending to a conclusion.
  Does the survivor sound personally involved without invented history?
    Yes. The notes observe and infer; nothing invents a memory or a consent.
  Does the institutional absurdity produce fatalistic comedy throughout?
    Yes, and it survived my edits: "Losing the event apparently doesn't
    qualify anyone for a more expensive bat."

CONCLUSION
  The changes read in place. No contradiction found between a rewritten
  finding and the records it sits between.
