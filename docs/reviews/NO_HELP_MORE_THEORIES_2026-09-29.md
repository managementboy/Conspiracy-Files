# No Help: more theories, two live per world

Design analysis, 2026-09-29. No code or content changes. Subsequent owner decisions in `DR-20260929-NOHELP-MORE-THEORIES` supersede this review's restricted-pair recommendation: every unordered pair in the available pool is eligible and equally likely, while pair coverage remains a prerequisite to adding a theory. Source: `nohelp-content` at `3fcfaf33`. Spoiler-free: theory letters, counts, file and function names only. A and B remain the existing theories; C denotes a proposed third. This is a recommendation, not a new owner decision. The current design documents' language about mutually contradictory accounts is superseded for the existing pair by `DR-20260929-NOHELP-B-OWN-EVIDENCE`: both may be true. The proposed pair-selection rule needs an explicit owner decision.

## Three overall approaches

| Approach | How it works | Writing and engine cost | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Universal theory library | Each clue belongs to a theory, with independent evidence of support for other theories. Select two from a reviewed pair graph at world creation; filter placement to those two. | One theory-wide content bank and pair-specific QA; picker, scene, trail and save changes. Growth is roughly linear in theories for core writing, quadratic in pair checks. | A broadly compatible theory swallows its partners, or a pair has thin evidence at some target. |
| Pair editions | Author a separate clue pool, interpretation and coverage plan for AB, AC, BC, etc.; pick an edition once per world. | Strong editorial control but duplicated targets and near-quadratic writing, receipts and maintenance. | An added theory multiplies work and makes the same target feel scripted. |
| Unusual: rotating evidence lenses | Keep one largely shared clue corpus, then choose two interpretations that make the same physical traces mean different things. The world selects the lens pair and placement weights, while wording stays fixed. | Relatively few new authored objects, but extensive pair-specific blind reads and contextual consistency checks. | Purely editorial relabeling makes replay cosmetic; hidden tags or altered captions could reveal the system and violate authored, cold-readable meaning. |

**Recommendation:** a universal theory library with an explicit compatibility and quality graph. A clue is authored once, its render is invariant, and a tested pair determines whether it is eligible. Permit overlapping explanations: a mystery is a contest of independently arguable accounts, not a requirement that one logically refute the other. Pair editions can remain a last resort for an exceptional incompatibility, without becoming the default content model. The rotating-lens approach fails the player's expectation that objects mean the same thing on a cold read.

## Pair selection and player experience

- At creation of a **new** world, draw one unordered pair from a versioned, reviewed allowlist using the world seed and a pair-selection salt. Start with AB, AC and BC only after all three pass pair gates. Equal weights initially; explicit integer weights can later tune exposure, but never silently exclude AB. Do not select from all mathematical pairs merely because their theories exist. For N theories there are N(N-1)/2 potential pairs, each requiring its own coverage and readability approval.
- Save the two stable theory IDs, the selected pair ID, selection-rule version and content catalog version before any area is decided. Do not derive the pair again on load or change it after an update. Keep selection independent of reading order, player action, occupancy and rerolls. A new world cannot be made eligible for a pair before that pair has a passing release manifest.
- Pair admission requires two complete, distinguishable arguments. Theories **may** overlap, as AB does, but every admitted pairing needs observations that give each side its own positive case and at least some observations or combinations that discriminate between them. Two theories that are synonyms, or one that merely adds an unobserved event to the other's complete story, should be withheld until their premises or evidence differ.
- Replay should change which causal patterns recur, the mix of evidence, and the interpretations a careful player can build at familiar marked targets. The player may infer that worlds vary; they must never see pair names, labels, lean tags, probabilities, a verdict, a belief meter, missing-theory placeholders or a fixed early clue that announces the pair. No journal, quest log or response conditioned on the player's beliefs. Keep enough redundant clues in varied routes that a player who misses early finds can still discover both live arguments.
- In particular, the B accessibility review finds 367 A-only, 166 B-only and 307 both among 840 one-read receipts at that snapshot. Compatibility is not independent support. Gate for evidence addressing each theory's own defining causal links; do not turn a generic institutional wrapper or a reader's invented missing premise into support for every pair. Preserve valid ambiguity, including both-support readings. This is an editorial and measurement problem as well as a picker problem.

## Authoring and eligibility

- Author a clue for a **primary theory**, not a fixed pair. Keep the existing physical content, `where[].lean`, and an author-side counter-reading. For new content, replace the implicit universal `where[].rival` requirement with a pair-keyed authoring relation or a separately reviewed applicability table. A concrete `rival` must refer to the *selected* partner when a new-world document is made. Do not claim that an existing A/B rival reading automatically works against C. The existing 840 rendered clues and their A/B metadata stay intact.
- Assign a blind-read support vector across theories, distinct from the writer's aim and from where-lean. A clue eligible for pair XY must have positive, item-specific support for X or Y in the approved pair read. If it only supports Z, do **not** spawn it, neutralize it, or reinterpret it as Y. It remains in the catalog for worlds containing Z. Ordinary vanilla material remains ordinary vanilla material.
- A clue read as **both** for XY can enter that pair, but does not by itself satisfy either side's *exclusive/diagnostic* quota. “Both” is a pair-relative observation, not a permanent property of the clue. A clue may be X-only in XY, both in XZ, and unrelated in YZ; do not store one global `both` bit. Multiple `where[]` rows can still provide placement options, but a location-dependent lean requires a separate pair read if the rendered place/spot changes materially.
- Freeze each new theory's axioms and record a counter-reading against each intended partner in the sidecar. Check chronology and causal chain, independent custody, a rival prediction and a genuine uncertainty across the bank. A pair-specific cross-read can admit an existing clue without rewriting it, but cannot change the text or override a blind `none` vote. Never make a clue's meaning depend on which partner was secretly selected.

### Size estimate, with the two different notions of coverage

Assume 253 distinct map/flyer **mark or note targets** and 125 eligible scene **kinds**, as supplied. A marked target requires at least three placed clues per world, with each live side present. A scene occurrence currently takes **one** clue, not three; its kind needs an eligible variant for either live side over worlds. Do not multiply the 125 scenes by three. The figures below count candidate authored rows and ignore additional ordinary-place stock, duplicated physical anchors, form ratio, and unusable spots.

| Requirement with AB, AC and BC eligible | Increment for C | Three-theory total at targets | Caveat |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| One distinct C candidate per each of 253 marked targets and 125 scene kinds | **378 rows** (253 + 125) | At least 759 mark-side candidates + 375 scene-side candidates = **1,134** if A and B also each have one at every target | This only guarantees *eligibility*: a reusable set or stock must fill the third marked-place slot; written clues cannot repeat. It does not ensure three different texts or success at every physical site. |
| Three **different** clues at each marked target for every pair, each pair containing both sides | **At least 506 C mark rows + 125 scene rows = 631**, if A and B already have at least two distinct candidates each at every target; otherwise add missing A/B rows | At least **5 distinct candidates per marked target = 1,265**, plus **375 scene-side candidates = 1,640** | For three theories, counts per target must be at least (2,2,1) in some order to make every pair sum to >=3. The conservative symmetrical bank is (2,2,2): 1,518 marked candidates + 375 scene candidates = 1,893, or **631 C additions** only if existing AB already has two each. |

Thus **378 is a theoretical entry floor**, not a production budget. **631 new C rows** is a useful distinct-evidence planning target (506 marks, 125 scene kinds), plus whatever A/B gaps, stock and ordinary-place coverage the audit finds. If two candidates for every theory at every marked target are required, C's increment is still 631; the total marked bank becomes 1,518. Counts are not “253 places × three clues × three pairs”: one authored candidate can serve both pairs containing its theory, and sets can be copied under current rules. Conversely, a single clue anchored to a target does not prove the live pair is covered there. Some keys lead to shared physical areas, so test mark-level and area-level constraints separately. Audit accepted anchors and actual seeded placement before turning these bounds into tickets.

## Blind read and gates

One independent model **session per clue** can ask about every theory the clue plausibly touches, but do not send N questions for every old clue by default. Retain the original A/B receipt and hash. For C, run one C question on the C bank and on the shortlisted A/B candidates likely to be shared; create pair-specific XY receipts only for rows actually offered to XY. The independent reader sees exactly the player-facing render and the two proposed premises, without aim, sidecar or other clues. Keep the production one-read rule; use a small, separately labeled two-reader sample to estimate drift, not a second vote per production clue. Cache by rendered hash, pair IDs and **prompt/premise version**; a changed render or premise invalidates the affected receipt, not unrelated pair receipts.

This makes read cost proportional to new and cross-eligible rows rather than N times the entire catalog. Each pair receipt has independent YES/NO support for its two theories, yielding X-only, Y-only, both or none. Record the observation or missing premise for QA. Test a sample using the B review's stricter distinction between direct or partial support and mere compatibility; do not retroactively overwrite the 840 old votes. A pair's accepted catalog excludes its `none` rows. For a multi-theory clue, one authoring ID may have several pair receipts; the engine only receives the pair's eligibility and chosen placement lean, never reader explanations.

For **every allowed pair**, measure and gate:

1. Anchor availability: every one of the 253 mark/note targets has eligible candidates for each live side, and every one of the 125 scene kinds has a candidate of each side at the required spot. Include scene-version variants and repeated-set fallback. Audit ordinary-place and spot types as `Manifest.lint` does.
2. Playable coverage: across a fixed, published seed suite and several content versions, `AreaCase.decide` supplies at least three per mark with both sides, fills the area count, and `AreaCase.decideScene` supplies one valid clue for either drawn side without silently replacing it with an off-pair clue. Check shared areas, anchor pool, global written exhaustion and repeated-set limits. Report numerator/denominator **per pair** and worst seed, not one pooled percentage.
3. Reader balance: compare X-only and Y-only receipts among pair-eligible clues, reporting both and none separately. Retain the current `targets.leanMax=0.55` as an initial *pair-specific candidate-pool* alarm, not an assertion that authored lean equals perceived support. Also simulate placed/encountered evidence; a balanced catalog can still yield a biased world through `Pick.choose`, `Trails.favour` or scene weights. Gate a lower bound for each side's diagnostic evidence in independent routes, alongside total count and form mix.
4. Narrative accessibility: a small blind play or reconstruction test asks whether each side has a coherent argument without a required clue, and whether neither side becomes an obvious canonical answer. Check repeat wording and physical objects across different pairs, not just within one.

**Order:** (1) freeze C axioms and the allowed-pair graph; (2) measure accepted anchors, blind support and per-pair target deficits without touching production; (3) author a small C bank and pair reads; (4) implement a versioned pair manifest and schema-2 case creation/validation, explicitly retaining schema-1 read/write behavior; (5) make picker, trail and scene paths consume the *saved* pair, with old AB functions untouched; (6) extend converter and progress gates; (7) test new worlds and existing-save continuation, then grow content to full coverage before enabling AC/BC. A pair not ready remains disabled; C may exist in the catalog without entering an old or unfinished world.

Existing saves have no pair field and `schemaVersion=1`; interpret them as **locked AB**, without rewriting old areas or documents. `AreaCase.validate` currently rejects unknown case fields and unsupported versions, while `AreaCase.grows` forbids changes to existing areas and documents. Therefore a schema-2 record must be a new-world shape or an explicitly designed compatible continuation, never a silent mutation of schema 1. Load old records through the original validation and pick/hash/scene paths. Existing 840 accepted A/B clues, their IDs, text, original receipt hashes and already placed copies remain unchanged. Later content updates can add eligible A/B clues to yet-undecided areas under the original behavior. A fresh world may draw AB using the new pair-aware path only after regression tests show old AB behavior or the owner accepts a deliberate new-world distribution change.

## Failure modes and safeguards

| Risk | Prevent it by |
| --- | --- |
| Replay feels identical because the same universal clues dominate | Require pair-diagnostic families, cap repeat copies in measured encounter routes, compare overlap of encountered clue IDs and causal links across seeded pairs; vary placement without changing clue wording. |
| Pair becomes obvious from a fixed opening, location category or item type | Audit early reachable evidence and containers by pair; mix affinity, include ordinary and shared traces, and never show hidden pair labels or completion status. |
| One theory swallows its partner | Demand independent causal links and pair-specific diagnostic readings; report both separately, test missing-premise reasoning, repair the thinner case by new evidence rather than false exclusivity. |
| Pair cannot be investigated because a mark has only off-pair clues or a scene has no live variant | Preflight all anchors and seeds per pair; deny activation until both-side coverage passes; never turn an off-pair clue into neutral filler. |
| A rare written clue is exhausted or a physical spot cannot host its intended piece | Test global ledger and runtime fallbacks, reserve reusable sets per side/place/spot, keep `short` visible to offline QA and allow the existing later-placement behavior without an in-game hint. |
| An update rerolls a pair or invalidates a decided area | Save pair and selection version atomically at creation; keep old schema dispatch and growth invariant; test save/load after content updates. |
| Player never sees enough of the new side | Evaluate practical route-level redundancy as well as total catalog ratios; expose both arguments in independent regions without guaranteeing a particular clue. |

## Smallest prototype and owner decisions

Prototype **C with AB, AC and BC** behind a development-only allowlist. Start with a handful of marked targets, a few scene kinds and ordinary places, writing enough distinct C evidence for both AC and BC. Blind-read C and shortlisted reusable A/B clues against both new partners; simulate many saved seeds, including shared targets, scene fallbacks, exhausted written clues and save reloads. Compare the three pair worlds in a spoiler-safe report of counts, support categories, missed targets and repeated-copy rates. This prototype does **not** make AC/BC playable at release until the full 253/125 coverage gate passes.

The owner must decide, in player terms:

1. **Are any two sufficiently distinct theories allowed to meet, or only selected combinations?** Recommendation: selected, tested combinations; AB, AC, BC are the first candidates, not automatic promises.
2. **Should the three pairs occur equally often?** Recommendation: yes initially, with an explicit option to change weights after playtesting.
3. **Is a third clue at a marked target allowed to be another copy of a set, or should all three be different discoveries?** This determines whether the 378-row floor is plausible or the 631+ distinct-evidence budget is appropriate. Recommendation: different discoveries when practical; repeated sets remain a resilience fallback.
4. **May two theories both be partly right?** Recommendation: yes, as for AB; each must still have its own arguable causal case.
5. **Should an existing world always remain its original A/B mystery after an update?** Recommendation: yes; new pair selection only for new worlds.
6. **May the player know that the pair changes between worlds, without being told which pair a given world drew?** Recommendation: explain this replay premise outside the mystery, never in the play UI.
