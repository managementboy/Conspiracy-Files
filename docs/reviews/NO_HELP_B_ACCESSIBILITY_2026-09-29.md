## Phase 1 — DIVERGE (five isolated frames; no evaluation)

Each frame starts from the same question: “Why do B clues read as A or both, and how could B be made to stand on its own?” Its six proposals were generated without referring to another frame. The first three obvious responses in each frame were discarded before listing these ideas.

### 1. Game designer

- **G1.** Let a player reconstruct a complete upstream chain from independent physical stages rather than collecting more single-stage B assertions.
- **G2.** Give each encounter a small, recognizable recurring variable whose changing value makes the B chain memorable across sessions.
- **G3.** Put a rival-looking response artifact beside a B-specific causal artifact so proximity invites comparison without an explanation screen.
- **G4.** Arrange redundant clues so B remains inferable when only one of several world routes is visited.
- **G5.** Make the most diagnostic B evidence physically ordinary and the apparently dramatic emergency evidence less diagnostic.
- **G6.** Use a recurring mismatch between predicted and observed distribution as the player's own test of the two accounts.

### 2. Detective / prosecutor

- **D1.** Establish a dated causal step before any response decision, then independently establish its link to later exposure.
- **D2.** Use a negative control: comparable nonexposed material follows normal operations while exposed material follows a distinct outcome.
- **D3.** Separate proof that a program existed from proof that its output traveled; the latter must have an identifiable trace.
- **D4.** Show an error repeated under the same operating condition, which an improvised response failure cannot itself generate.
- **D5.** Include ordinary, contemporaneous correction attempts so absence of concealment is a testable rival claim rather than an author's assurance.
- **D6.** Require the B argument to name one observation that would be surprising if the response alone caused the pattern.

### 3. Inversion (failure recipe, then its negation)

- **I1.** Guarantee failure by starting every B lead after official intervention; negate it by anchoring a subset before intervention.
- **I2.** Guarantee failure by putting every B trace in an altered institutional record; negate it by giving some traces independent physical custody.
- **I3.** Guarantee failure by using missing information as the whole B argument; negate it with positive observations that remain meaningful if records are complete.
- **I4.** Guarantee failure by making a response actor explain the program's mechanism; negate it with operational traces from before that actor could know.
- **I5.** Guarantee failure by allowing any unexplained exposure to count as program exposure; negate it with a constrained pathway and a failed alternative prediction.
- **I6.** Guarantee failure by making B require secrecy; negate it with a publicly visible, routine process whose unintended effect is the disputed fact.

### 4. Real-world conspiracy culture

- **C1.** Give B believers a repeatable “same input, same outcome” pattern to argue about, not merely a suspicious institution.
- **C2.** Let the theory spread through competing interpretations of an ordinary technical anomaly rather than a confession.
- **C3.** Build B's folklore around a disputed measurement that anyone could copy incorrectly or honestly.
- **C4.** Make a mundane distribution network the alleged amplifier, leaving response politics as a separate dispute.
- **C5.** Let believers disagree internally over which step failed while agreeing on a traceable program-origin chain.
- **C6.** Give a skeptical person a mundane counterexample that believers must explain, instead of only accumulating corroboration.

### 5. Linguist / blind reader

- **L1.** Replace retrospective institutional verbs with contemporaneous process observations where the evidence really supports them.
- **L2.** Distinguish “the response could hide this” from “this item independently indicates the response was mismanaged and hidden.”
- **L3.** Put the diagnostic relation before the bureaucratic wrapper in a clue's sentence order.
- **L4.** Avoid a final sentence that interprets a technical irregularity as an institutional motive.
- **L5.** Test whether a B reading survives when every agency label is removed but the physical relation remains.
- **L6.** Ask the reader for its strongest item-specific support and missing premise separately, so a generic compatible story is visible.

### b) Clusters by underlying angle

1. **Upstream causal chain and chronology:** G1, D1, D3, D6, I1, I4, C4. The evidence should connect origin, movement, and effect before response claims enter.
2. **Controls and discriminating predictions:** G6, D2, D4, I5, C1, C3, C6. Comparable cases and failed predictions give B more than mere compatibility.
3. **Independent custody and ordinary process:** G5, D5, I2, I3, I6, C2. A physical or routine trace has less dependence on an alleged suppression story.
4. **Player discovery and resilience:** G2, G3, G4, C5. Recognition must survive unpredictable order and incomplete coverage without a belief interface.
5. **Language and measurement scope:** L1–L6. Text can add motive where the underlying item has only a technical relation, while a reader can grant a theory support for mere compatibility.

### c) Deepen the top three

**G1 — reconstructable upstream chain (8.65).** Author a small family of independent observations that connect a program step, a departure from intended operation, and later exposure. Each clue must make sense alone; finding several lets the player infer the connection in either order. Give each step its own rival reading, and do not have a character announce the chain's conclusion. This moves the question from whether something suspicious happened to whether the program could actually have caused it before the response. **Risk:** a perfectly tidy chain becomes a solved puzzle or a repeated template. **First step:** audit B-aimed clues for which causal link each independently supplies, using IDs and sidecars, then choose one thin link for a small prototype. **Children:** a dated precursor; an independent transfer trace; a downstream pattern; a near-match that weakens the chain; a second route to the same inference.

**L2 — separate compatibility from independent response support (8.65).** Keep the two independent YES/NO questions, but require the reader to point to a clue-specific observation for *mismanaged response plus concealment* before voting A. A response believer may invent a compatible explanation for almost anything, and that is different from evidence for A's conjunctive claim. Apply the same standard to B: program presence alone is insufficient unless an item supports a malfunction or a pathway. Record compatibility separately from direct support in a measurement experiment, while leaving the production gate and existing receipts intact until results are compared. **Risk:** a stricter prompt can relabel ambiguous clues without making the player's case for B any stronger. **First step:** blind-reread a fixed stratified sample under both prompts, with separate fresh sessions and no writer tags. **Children:** explicit missing-premise field; independent-support vote; compatible-but-nondiagnostic vote; inter-reader agreement check; disagreement audit by form.

**D2 — negative controls and discriminating predictions (8.60).** Create comparable observations with one relevant difference and let a B believer predict which should diverge. A response-cover-up believer can still invent an additional story, but the paired pattern itself is upstream and does not depend on hidden official action. Spread the halves so either is an ordinary clue alone and their relation rewards recall rather than order. Include imperfect controls so the result suggests a causal account without becoming a proof of origin. **Risk:** too neat a comparison implies a canonical solution and may require the player to find both halves. **First step:** draft one control pair and blind-read each half and the pair, tracking whether B-only support emerges from the relation. **Children:** matched input; unexposed comparator; contrary instance; dose or timing gradient; independent replication.

## Direct answers

### Cause and evidence

**The formulation is the primary structural cause; writing habits and the current question amplify it.** A describes a mishandled *response* plus a concealed failure, while B describes an *origin* and currently adds its own obscured-record axiom (`a-obscured`). Those can both be true. They are rival emphases, not logically incompatible accounts. The older vision's different worked pair cannot repair the current definition; the current design explicitly selected this pair. Because a program-origin event may coexist with a failed and concealed response, no honest item can logically prove B and rule out every possible A story. Aim instead for B-diagnostic evidence that gives no independent reason to assert A. Do not manufacture an absolute “only B” proof.

**Writing adds a strong, measurable skew.** B-aimed written clues read B-only in 40/210 (19%) and both in 165/210 (79%); B-aimed sets read B-only in 121/249 (49%) and both in 101/249 (41%). By comparison, A-aimed written clues read A-only in 114/125 (91%). Among B-aimed bodies below 240 characters, 124/254 read B-only; at 240 characters or more, 37/205 do. Form and length are entangled and the sample is not randomized, so this does not establish that shortening alone fixes the problem. B-only long exceptions exist, including `t0120-02`, `t0120-05`, and `t0048-06`. The neighboring IDs `t0120-01` (B aim, A read) and `t0120-02`/`t0120-05` (B aim, B read) show the problem is not a ticket-wide fate. `t0421-10` is another B-aimed A read. The set exceptions `t0001-02`, `t0291-01`, and `t0417-01` are B-only. These IDs identify audit targets without exposing their content.

**The axioms and recipes need sharper roles.** In B-aimed sidecars, the common `a-program` + `a-pathway` pairing has 89 B-only, 204 both, and 31 A-only reads; sidecar tags describe writer reasoning, not a verified causal mechanism in the rendered clue. `a-malfunction` and `a-timing` are much less frequently tagged in the accepted sidecars than `a-pathway`, so the intended origin claim is often carried by a broad pathway. Recipes specify side and form, but the blind check accepts all A/B/both results; a B aim is therefore a production target, not a requirement that the item discriminate. The general stock, map/flyer-place, and scene figures supplied in the question show the skew across all three groups, so a single placement category is not its whole cause. No content needs to be discarded to make this diagnosis.

**The read question is a secondary measurement cause.** “Could a person who already believes A point to this item?” allows an imaginative believer to add an unseen cover-up to a B item. Its instruction to name something in the item limits that move, but does not ask whether that thing actually supports both parts of A. The asymmetry is real: 266/459 B-aimed clues become both, against 41/381 A-aimed clues. The one-read sample has no independent reliability estimate. A revised prompt should be tested in parallel, never used retroactively to declare old receipts wrong.

### Alternative formulations of B

1. **Upstream exposure chain.** B says a routine experimental program suffered an operational deviation before the emergency response and spread exposure along a traceable distribution path; the subsequent response can have been imperfect, but that is neither a premise nor the evidence for the origin claim. A believer argues from linked precursor, deviation, transfer, and outcome observations, with controls that make response-only explanations poor fits. **Axiom change:** retain `a-program`, narrow `a-malfunction` and `a-pathway` to observable links, strengthen `a-timing` into a before-response constraint, and remove `a-obscured` as a required fact. This is the most faithful repair of the selected premise, though the two theories can still coexist in reality.

2. **Open technical failure.** B says the program was known to its operators, a repeatable technical error escaped ordinary controls, and attempts to correct it were real but insufficient; the decisive dispute is the failed process, not suppressed responsibility for emergency response. A believer can cite a reproducible operating condition, a comparator, and a correction that failed to stop exposure without alleging falsified public records. **Axiom change:** make `a-malfunction` specify a repeatable operating condition and `a-pathway` an independently observable consequence; replace `a-obscured` with a neutral uncertainty or incomplete-diagnosis axiom, explicitly excluding intentional response concealment *from B's required account*. Absence of concealment must not be announced as proven by one clue.

3. **Distribution-first error.** B says a standardized program input propagated through ordinary channels before its unintended effect was recognized; the signature is differential exposure along that channel, including unaffected comparators. Its believer argues from distribution and outcome, not from a later decision to hide a response failure. **Axiom change:** define `a-program` as a specific pre-event input, tighten `a-pathway` around traceable distribution, make `a-timing` and comparison observations load-bearing, and drop `a-obscured`. This formulation should remain a malfunction, not quietly become a deliberate attack or a promise of a known biological mechanism.

These formulations make B independently *arguable*, not metaphysically exclusive of A. If literal mutual contradiction is mandatory, A must also be narrowed or B must make a falsifiable counterclaim about the response. That is a separate owner-level premise choice, not a wording trick to impose on individual clues.

### Fairer blind-read experiment

Preserve the existing production prompt and receipts for continuity. In a separate, preregistered sample, ask for each side: “Which observation in this item independently supports the defining causal claim, and which necessary premise is missing? Mark **direct support**, **compatible only**, or **no usable support**.” For A, the defining claim is response mismanagement **and** concealment; for B, it is program malfunction **and** a plausible exposure pathway preceding the outbreak. Allow a clue to support one component, but label that as partial instead of quietly promoting it to the whole theory. Keep the reader blind to aim, sidecar, and other clues; compare changes by form and body length, and run a second independent reader on the sample to estimate stability. Report both the old inclusive YES rate and the new direct-support rate. This is a measurement experiment, not a new gate that rejects useful ambiguity.

### Wildcard provocation

Make the strongest B argument an **absence pattern**: a set of ordinary, comparable traces exists where the program's input was absent, while the disputed effect follows where it was present. The player discovers the relationship without a quest, journal, or verdict. It risks looking like a laboratory proof, so one counterexample should remain genuinely troublesome to B.

## Suggested decision

Prototype the upstream-chain formulation and one negative-control relation, then test a stratified sample with the revised read question. Keep the existing 840 clues and their receipts as the baseline. Decide whether the project truly needs logically contradictory theories or two live, competing explanations with potentially compatible components before revising frozen axioms.
