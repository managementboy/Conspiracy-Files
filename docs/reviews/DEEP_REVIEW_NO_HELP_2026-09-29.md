# Deep review — Conspiracy Files: No Help

**Branch/revision reviewed:** `nohelp-content` at `384e99a5` (2026-09-29). **Scope:** `mod-nohelp/`, 518 accepted clue rows and sidecars, receipts, converter, progress tool, and the relevant design and handoff documents. This is a static and data review, not a native Build 42 playtest. All clue references below are opaque IDs.

## Promises to the player

The current decisions supersede the retired premise and earlier proposed front end. In my words, the mod promises:

1. **Two live arguments:** A and B can each make a serious case from evidence the player can encounter; neither is declared true.
2. **Physical discovery:** at least half the placed findings are meaningful sets of real vanilla objects, and their physical arrangement matters.
3. **No Help:** the player does the interpreting, without a case screen, quest log, belief score, answer, or automatic map annotations supplied by the mod. Search Mode's local icon and the nearby cue are the expressly allowed finding signals.
4. **Every clue is readable:** Search Mode spotting recognises a clue; a looted clue has a working “Look it over” fallback; the clue's authored information can then be encountered in play.
5. **Vanilla marks matter:** every map mark and flyer place offers an investigation grounded in its own annotation, with multiple clues and a rival reading; a map read has no deadline.
6. **Vanilla scenes matter:** an actually confirmed story scene gets one fitting, reachable clue beside its own objects, without overwriting the scene.
7. **No geography or object lookup table:** placement varies by world and route, keeps both arguments available, and does not teach a repetitive title, object, or spot as a shortcut.
8. **Fair world history:** decisions and spots persist before reveal; reload and death do not reroll or respawn consumed evidence; unfound complete clues can move only safely within their area.
9. **Quiet, robust play:** bounded background work, reasonable save cost as the world grows, safe handling of vanished objects and other mods, and a clean single-player-only boundary.
10. **Independent content gate:** authored clues, provenance and counter-readings pass structural checks and a genuine blind reader; progress means playable coverage, not merely populated files.

## Findings by promise

Severity describes the risk **before a real content playtest**. “Blocker” means the advertised loop is unavailable or a large content family cannot enter play; it does not mean the package fails to boot.

### 4. Every clue is readable

**Blocker — the clue inventory actions are unreachable.** `mod-nohelp/common/media/lua/client/NHShared/GeneratedRuntime.lua:1009-1012` makes `R.subject(item)` return `false,false` unconditionally. `GeneratedMenu.lua:68-70` rejects the item before constructing either action, and `ClueActions.lua:75-77,109-111` independently requires `R.subject(item)==true`. Search Mode can still call `R.recognise(id,"search")` (`ClueSearch.lua:304-315`) and stamp a title; this does not make the menu usable. In particular, the stated fallback for an item picked up before searching cannot run, and neither can the deliberate inspection/discovery path. **Player impact:** a survivor can collect or spot clues without accessing the intended action that records and explains them. **Fix:** implement a token/assignment checked subject predicate and test the *actual menu and timed action* for a placed item, before and after recognition, both carried and in place. The existing `test/nohelp_found_how.lua` calls `Session.recognise` directly and does not exercise this path.

**Blocker — most set captions have no visible reading surface.** The converter places `body` in every set, but placement writes it only through `writePages` when an item implements `addPage` (`GeneratedRuntime.lua:136-146,320-340`). Recognition changes item names/categories, not their body (`:1048-1060`); `EvidenceRows.lua:47-115` is an old organiser projection, while `InteractionAPI.lua:8-11` explicitly says the organiser is absent. `GeneratedMenu.open` still describes the old organiser, but there is no caller giving these sets a readable caption. At least **212/349** sets have no piece with even a paper-like item ID; this is a conservative lexical screen, not an engine type proof. Examples to verify include `t0260-01`, `t0283-01`, `t0291-01`. **Player impact:** the blind reader assessed title, body, pieces and spot together, while a player often gets the objects and later a title without the authored observation that made them meaningful. **Fix:** provide one player-facing, in-world way to read the set's short caption after recognition, with no theory tag or ledger; test non-literature and mixed sets in the native game. Review the captions again against that exact presentation.

### 5 and 1. Vanilla marks and two live arguments

**Blocker — an anchored site with one clue cannot satisfy its minimum or its rival.** `AreaCase.anchorPool` (`Generated/AreaCase.lua:236-249`) returns *only* clues anchored to that site's keys whenever at least one exists. `Pick.choose` (`Generated/Pick.lua:75-175`) can repeat sets only if the pool offers them and cannot create an opposite lean. In the accepted data, **206 of 217 distinct map anchor keys** and **128 of 133 flyer anchor keys** have just one declared lean; the whole accepted list has 228 map-anchored and 138 flyer-anchored rows. Several keys can merge into one physical area, so these are anchor-key counts, not an asserted count of deficient runtime areas. Isolated sites nonetheless have a maximum of one unique row (or one copy of its set within that area), despite the 2–10 target, the marked-site minimum of three, and one of each side. `AreaCase.decide` stores a positive `short` rather than refusing (`:273-306`). **Player impact:** a map can lead to a single beat, without the promised counter-evidence, even though progress reports complete map coverage. **Fix:** run `Pick` with the *real* accepted manifest over every actual `MapSites` grouping and both world-favour outcomes; require minimum count and both leans, then write suitable anchored additions or make a deliberate, location-valid fallback available. Count physical areas rather than ticket keys.

**Serious — the scene's randomly selected lean often has no authored version.** `VanillaScenes.lua:266-279` chooses a lean by world seed. `AreaCase.decideScene` (`:324-355`) requires a clue for that exact scene kind, spot and lean, otherwise returns `empty`. The accepted 128 scene rows cover 125 kinds, but **124 of 126 distinct scene anchor forms** are single-lean, and the scene rows' declared split is **116 B / 12 A**. A single-lean scene therefore has a world-dependent chance of receiving no clue even when observed correctly. **Player impact:** the “one clue per confirmed scene” promise fails silently on one of the two world outcomes for most kinds. **Fix:** provide both versions for every allowed kind or choose only among available versions with an explicit, tested balance rule; test every kind under both selected leans.

### 6. Vanilla scenes matter

**Blocker — accepted scene coverage is mostly unreachable by the matcher.** `Generated/SceneMatch.lua:43-89,135-149,196-220` has only **three verified signatures**; unverified kinds deliberately never match. `Generated/VanillaScenes.lua:196-252` has one separate fixed citation. Yet the 128 accepted scene clues are counted as coverage by anchor presence (`tools/nohelp_content/progress.lua:94-113`) without asking whether their scenes can be recognised. For example `t0283-01`, `t0291-01`, `t0295-01` pass the content pipeline but belong to kinds without a matching signature. **Player impact:** more than a hundred scene-kind tickets are mainly dormant in ordinary random play; nearby place clues do not make these scene-specific clues appear. **Fix:** either verify reliable signatures for the intended first-release subset and limit progress to it, or clearly defer the other scene tickets. Measure detections and false positives in a visible native run. Do not relax the exclusive-trace requirement merely to inflate the count.

**Serious — scene fit is checked by label/spot, not by observable staging.** `Manifest.validAnchor` (`:131-151`) checks scene identity and spot class; `convert.lua:285-350` checks schema, recipe and merged lint. Neither proves that a caption's claimed neighbouring props, wear or sequence exists in that particular vanilla scene. `t0283-01`, `t0291-01`, `t0295-01`, `t0287-01` and `t0299-01` reuse observations involving an additional, unlisted prop or trace that the clue's `pieces` do not create. **Player impact:** when such a clue does appear, the caption can tell the survivor about an object that is not there, turning an observation into invented evidence. **Fix:** audit each scene clue against the cited vanilla implementation and its actual generated pieces; either make the claim an explicitly written assertion on paper or ensure the physical trace is placed and stable. Add a scene-citation review field whose existence is checked, then verify samples in game.

### 2 and 7. Physical discovery without a lookup table

**Serious — set semantics often reside in prose instead of the set.** All **349** sets are formally 2–4 vanilla items, but many captions describe timing, condition, ownership or an absent third prop that the pair alone cannot demonstrate; `t0260-01`, `t0287-01`, `t0291-01` are concrete examples. The physical items do not acquire the claimed environmental state simply because the JSON body describes it (`GeneratedRuntime.lua:320-342`; `applyWear` only uses explicit wear fields, which the accepted game-field list does not include). **Player impact:** the nominal 67.4% physical share overstates how much of the story can be learned by observing the world. **Fix:** distinguish what the placed objects visibly establish from what a witness wrote; rewrite or stage assertions that require a third prop, condition or temporal inference. Blind-test with the actual player-visible surface, excluding sidecars.

**Serious — one title pattern is a strong meta-clue.** **73/518** accepted titles start with the same four-word construction; **15** more use another repeated four-word construction. The common title is especially concentrated on sets (`t0260-01`, `t0198-01`, `t0147-01`). `convert.lua:291-294` rejects only a duplicate of the *entire normalised title plus body*; checklist E2/E3's drift detector is still unchecked. Object pairs also recur: the leading pair occurs **18** times, the next **12**, then **9** and **8** (190 unique unordered pairs among 349 sets). **Player impact:** after recognition, the title becomes a manufactured fingerprint; repeated pairs and phrasing reduce the pleasure of noticing a unique physical contradiction. **Fix:** batch-review title stems, five-word body patterns, pairs and spot distribution; revise the concentrated families before playtest and add a warning-only drift report.

**Serious — ground is the default rather than one option.** **402/518** rows have `ground` as their only spot, including **280** sets; only 62 use furniture, 27 vehicles, 21 bodies and 6 mailboxes. Every row in this snapshot has one `where` entry. `Session.intentMatches` and the filler (`GeneratedRuntime.lua:1848-1959`) preserve that exact spot type, so runtime randomness does not change the distribution. **Player impact:** map and flyer investigations may feel like repeated loose piles, while the intended furniture/body/vehicle affordances scarcely contribute. **Fix:** diversify compatible `where` entries where the wording really works in each spot, and author spot-specific variants where it does not; measure *actually placed and found* spot shares in a native route.

### 3. No Help

**Serious — a leftover automatic map annotation system conflicts with the stated experience.** `ClueMarkers.lua:14-25,143-206,226-326,344-363` records a picked-up or inspected clue's source, turns it into a question-mark and title on the world map when the survivor carries a writing tool, and hooks map rendering in ordinary single-player. It also has a **64-record ceiling** (`:40-48`), below a long world's discovery volume. The current broken menu limits in-place records but pickups can still be recorded. **Player impact:** the mod supplies a persistent indexed location aid contrary to “no markers”; after 64 records the marker worker may stop and log a refusal. **Fix:** remove this feature from the shipped No Help interaction path if the no-markers decision is authoritative, while preserving any internal count-only telemetry needed for tests. Test a long world with more than 64 finds.

**Minor — the development dump appears in ordinary play.** `StateDumpTrigger.lua:10-16,51-63` gates on single-player and modes, not debug; the previous review's F-03 is **still open**. **Player impact:** a diagnostic menu entry intrudes on an ordinary world context menu, although its output is content-blind. **Fix:** use a verified development gate or remove the trigger from the shipped package; test debug on/off.

### 8 and 9. Stable history, saves and quiet play

**Serious — a partly created set can become permanently unknown.** `GeneratedRuntime.lua:228-345`: intent is saved as `placing` before adding pieces; a reload retries only when the count is zero and the clue was unseen, while a nonzero incomplete count takes `unknown`. The source also marks failed item creation unknown. This is safer than duplication, but the missing pieces are never supplied. The provisional K3 rule and the real save-window force-kill check remain open (`NO_HELP_DEV_CHECKLIST_2026-09-28.md`, B4/D1). **Player impact:** a crash or item failure can permanently remove one of a world's finite findings. **Fix:** measure the native save window, then implement a token-and-piece-aware recovery decision with a regression for partial sets; do not silently replace a set that a player already saw.

**Serious — every world-record mutation scales with all prior play.** `Generated/Session.lua:541-566` copies, growth-checks, validates and copies the world record into the sink; `api.snapshot()` deep-copies it again. `GeneratedRuntime.lua:1819-1840,2310-2390` schedules repeated scans and placement work; `VanillaSceneRuntime.lua:299-317` periodically selects flagged cells. The existing synthetic soak reported **53 ms at 10×** after the partial optimisation; the growth gate is report-only and native late-save cost is unchecked (checklist A3–A7/D2). **Player impact:** stutter and memory allocation increase as hundreds of clues accumulate, possibly during exploration or saves. **Fix:** collect frame and save-window data in a long native save; then apply keyed commits/compaction with migration if the measured cost warrants it. Keep the per-frame budgets and scene queue telemetry in the acceptance run.

**Minor — recognised items outside the survivor's bags can lose their Evidence category after reload.** `GeneratedRuntime.lua:2392-2417` restamps only the player's inventory tree. A recognised clue left in a world container can retain its name while displaying as ordinary loot. The checklist's earlier handoff records this as an open native check. **Player impact:** revisiting a stored clue may obscure its recognised state. **Fix:** restamp lazily on container exposure or when resolving the live assignment; confirm category behaviour in the native game.

**Minor — multiplayer is deliberately disabled, but needs a clean boundary test.** `GeneratedRuntime.lua:78-81`, `MapMediaRuntime.lua:31`, `VanillaSceneRuntime.lua:61-65` and the interactive modules gate client/server execution. **Player impact:** a dedicated server should not produce half-owned evidence, but this has not been demonstrated by a dedicated-server start. **Fix:** smoke-test server and client co-install, confirming no placement, menus or persistent partial record; present single-player-only as an explicit compatibility limit.

### 10. Independent content gate and progress

**Serious — “DONE-CANDIDATE” is an inventory check, not a playable-coverage check.** `progress.lua:94-113` asks only whether each requested anchor key appears. Its sets/balance gates (`:130-164`) count accepted forms and one blind-reader vote, not actual placement, visibility or scene reachability. `test/nohelp_playthrough.lua` explicitly uses a synthetic placeholder inventory rather than the 518 accepted clues (placement plan §8). **Player impact:** all tickets can be green while most scene clues cannot trigger and many marked sites cannot satisfy the picker. **Fix:** run the route harness against the shipped manifest, enumerate real site pools and both scene leans, and make the candidate status fail on minimums, shortfalls and reachable scene share.

**Serious — the blind reader answers a weaker question than the design needs.** `check_receipts.lua:35-45` renders title, body, pieces and abstract `place / spot`, omitting the actual vanilla mark, flyer text, scene state and runtime presentation. `:59-83` checks hash, vote shape and non-`none`, but cannot verify reader independence or the truth of `model`; a manually written valid receipt would pass. All **518** have receipts: **165 A-only, 186 B-only, 167 both, 0 none**. Those counts show one model found an argument, not that a player receives the same information or that a “both” clue holds up in context. **Fix:** maintain the one-read rule, but show the reader exactly what the player can inspect, add a separate cited context-fit and physical-feasibility review, and have CI verify receipt provenance from an immutable run artifact rather than a self-described JSON field.

**Minor — the authoring gates do protect useful structural invariants.** `Manifest.validAnchor`, `Manifest.lint`, `ClueGates` and the converter catch item IDs, shape, recalled pieces, basic citations and exact duplicate title/body. Their limits are semantic rather than absence of checks. No sampled clue literally declares the winner; a corpus scan found no direct “proof”/“caused”/theory-name declaration. This is a bounded observation, not proof that every inference stays subtle.

## What is solid

- The runtime has one persisted world record, a world seed and append-only area decisions (`GeneratedRuntime.bootstrap`, `AreaCase.decide`, `Session.open`). The picker does not take a belief input. Its place and scene ledgers are separate, so a scene does not rewrite nearby area picks.
- The staged placement path saves a target before creating items, stamps members with a common physical token, counts expected pieces, refuses a known conflict and does not reroll a consumed clue. Search Mode keeps one icon per clue and persists “shown” to prevent subsequent moves.
- The three verified scene signatures use two trace sorts and an exclusive trace; demoting ambiguous signatures is prudent. The observer bounds squares per job and saves confirmed scenes once. These are credible safeguards, pending native timing evidence.
- The accepted list has **518 rows: 349 sets (67.4%) and 169 written**. There are 518 sidecar entries and 518 one-vote receipts. The prior review's version-minimum and optional vehicle-scan findings are resolved in the current code (`42/mod.info:6`, `Generated/Storage.lua:150-163`). The state dump itself is count-only; its menu gate remains open.
- The existing offline tests cover picker determinism, area validation, reload kill points in a fake world, sets, ground spots, map sites, a sample of scene matching and synthetic routes. They do **not** prove the inventory action is reachable, actual clue text is readable, the full accepted manifest meets per-site minima, the unverified scene kinds trigger, native map-chunk save ordering, or late-game frame times.

## Five fixes before a real content playtest

1. Restore the actual clue menu/action path and make every set's observation readable on an appropriate physical surface; exercise it with non-literature items in game.
2. Run the shipped 518-clue manifest through every real marked-site pool; fill short and one-sided pools without exposing a hidden theory label.
3. Resolve the scene gap: provide a reachable first-release subset with reliable signatures and both lean versions, and align progress with that subset.
4. Audit the concentrated title/spot/object patterns and scene captions against visible props; revise the identified IDs and run a player-view blind read.
5. Remove automatic world-map clue annotations from No Help, then perform the visible long-save, top-speed scene, partial-save and co-install acceptance run recorded in the checklist.

## Method and limits

Read the named design, placement, vision, handoff, checklist, decisions and prior review first. Traced the client event entry points, `GeneratedRuntime`/`Session`/`AreaCase`/`Pick`, printed-media and scene observer, Search Mode, cue, menu and save paths. Parsed all 395 accepted ticket JSON files and all 518 receipts with a read-only Python audit; inspected clustered samples and their sidecars. Counts above refer to this exact checkout.
