# Frame 1 — response-account axioms
Prompt: Treat the response-failure account on its own. Identify the smallest set of non-negotiable facts it needs, keep outbreak origin outside its remit, and avoid writing fictional evidence or clue rows.

- Keep the theory about choices made after danger was recognized; do not make the same theory explain the biological origin.
- Distinguish a warning problem from an operational problem: decision-makers can lack reliable information, and controls can still fail after decisions are made.
- Include the civilian consequence of failed movement or protection. Administrative embarrassment alone is not an account of an outbreak response.
- Treat later record handling as a separate possible fact from the original operational failure; otherwise every gap becomes circular proof of concealment.
- Preserve ordinary institutional routines as context. A form can remain complete while its procedure has become useless.
- Leave intent open. Mismanagement and subsequent minimization are enough; a centrally planned release is not required.
- Candidate structure: response boundary; incomplete warning; control/evacuation failure; institutional record narrowing. Keep the cause of the outbreak unspecified.
