# Frame 2 — agricultural-account axioms
Prompt: Treat the agricultural-program-malfunction account on its own. Identify the minimum causal chain it needs, avoid asserting a particular organism or deliberate release, and do not write fictional evidence or clue rows.

- A program must be more than a label: an experimental agricultural or pesticide activity existed before the event.
- A malfunction needs a bridge from activity to harm: handling, application, storage, or controls failed in a way that allowed unintended exposure.
- The account needs a plausible route from a bounded program to people beyond its immediate work site; otherwise it explains an incident, not the wider outbreak.
- Keep timing as a necessary relationship, not as a precise date or technical mechanism that the game cannot support.
- Make concealment or misclassification an aftermath possibility, not the malfunction itself; separate cause from institutional self-protection.
- Do not require malice, a specific pathogen, or proof that every exposed person encountered the same material.
- Candidate structure: program existed; controls failed; exposure travelled; timing supports a causal reading; institutional descriptions obscure the connection.
