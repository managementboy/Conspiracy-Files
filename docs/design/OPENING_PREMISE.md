# The opening premise — "No contact at premises"

Design document. Nothing here is built. The premise and evidence sequence for
the personal opening mystery (`DR-20260919-Q31`, and part B of
[the development plan](../management/DEVELOPMENT_TIMELINE_2026-09-19.md)).

Written payoff-first, per `DR-20260919-PAYOFF-SCOPE`: the conclusion is settled
below before a single clue is designed, and each clue exists to earn part of it.

---

## What the player ends up knowing

> A transfer out of the zone was scheduled in the survivor's own name. It was
> recorded against a house number one door from theirs. Someone went to that
> door, found nobody, and struck the survivor's name from the list as **"no
> contact at premises."** Someone with official access had also been at the
> survivor's own house before the power failed.

That is the local payoff: concrete, earned, sourced, and about *this* survivor.
It answers "why am I still here" in the most banal way available, which is the
agreed tone (`DR-20260919-Q24`, fatalistic bureaucratic dark comedy).

**What it contributes to the wider search** — and deliberately does not settle:
was the number a clerical error, or was the record altered? Who was at the
house, and on whose authority? The case ends holding those two open questions,
not answers to them. Nothing here explains Knox, and nothing implies an
explanation exists (`DR-20260919-CONSISTENT-EVENTS`).

## The four things the opening must establish

| Requirement | How this premise meets it |
|---|---|
| A personal connection to the starting place | The slip carries the survivor's own name; the address is their street. Their belongings are in the house. |
| A question about how or why they arrived | Not amnesia — the sharper question is why they were *left*. A transfer existed and did not reach them. |
| A local payoff contributing to the wider search | The struck line, named and dated, plus evidence of an official visit. |
| Skill observations as an additional layer | The electrician reads the meter; everyone else still finishes the case. |

## Modest personal facts used

Only what the game already supplies, plus one placed object.

- The survivor's **name**, from character creation.
- The survivor's **house number and street** — the shipped whole-map address
  book already knows both (`P4-R129`).
- The survivor's **own belongings** in the starting house.
- One **note we place ourselves** — no dependency on randomly spawned vanilla
  media.

No employment history, no backstory, no relatives, no prior life invented.

---

## The evidence sequence

Five findings. The case completes on **three**, by more than one route, so an
interruption or a missed clue is recoverable. Every finding is separated into
what was **observed**, what a document **claims**, and what the two together
**support** (`DR-20260919-SUPPORT`).

### 1 — The retained slip *(starting house, placed by us)*

A resident transfer slip in the survivor's own name. A date, a reference code,
and an address. Bureaucratic furniture: *"RETAIN THIS SLIP. Do not present it
at the assembly point."*

- **Observed:** a slip bearing your name, among your own things.
- **Claims:** a transfer was scheduled for that address on that date.
- **Supports:** someone wrote a slip in your name. Nothing yet about whether
  any transfer happened.

### 2 — The address that isn't this house *(no skill; the universal route)*

Comparing the slip's number against the number on the door. One digit out.

- **Observed:** your house is one number; the slip names another on the same
  street.
- **Supports:** the slip does not name this house.
- **Does not support:** that this was a mistake. An error and an alteration
  look identical from here — and the case must never claim to know which.

### 3 — The neighbouring door *(either this or finding 4 will do)*

At the number the slip names: a completed counterfoil, a chalk mark, a notice
still pinned.

- **Observed:** a completed stub at the other number, dated.
- **Claims:** the round ran on this street that day.
- **Supports:** a transfer party did reach this street. It reached the wrong
  door.

### 4 — The desk copy *(either this or finding 3 will do)*

A clipboard or carbon at a small official place — a post office counter, a
police foyer, a warden's table. A struck line carrying the same reference code.

- **Observed:** your reference struck through, annotated.
- **Claims:** *"no contact at premises."*
- **Supports:** someone at a desk closed the record against you. **Not** who,
  and **not** why. An unsigned annotation stays an annotation that was found;
  it never becomes an established decision by being written down.

### 5 — The house had a visitor *(the skill layer)*

- **With electrical skill:** the meter was pulled at the box before the grid
  failed, not by the failure. **Supports:** someone with access was at this
  house, before.
- **Without it, the same conclusion by another road:** the door was opened with
  a key rather than forced, or a delivery mark sits on the frame.
  **Supports:** the same thing, observed differently.

The skill changes *how the survivor notices*, never *whether the case can
finish*. Other occupations get their own reading of the same fact, which is what
makes this a pilot for the remaining twenty-four rather than a one-off.

## Recovery, and refusing to play

- **Interrupted or missed:** findings 3 and 4 are alternative roads to the same
  supported claim. Finding 5 has a skilled and an unskilled road. Three
  findings complete the case.
- **Deliberate refusal:** no ending is forced. The slip sits in the organiser
  as an open lead, and the case waits. A player who never investigates is not
  handed a conclusion.
- **Never:** a case that cannot reach its essential evidence must say so rather
  than quietly complete (`DR-20260919-SOLVABLE-WITHDRAWN`).

## What the follow-up inherits

The sourced finding — *a transfer in your name was struck as no contact* — and
the two open questions: whether the number was wrong or made wrong, and who had
access to the house. The follow-up may **challenge** the reading: a second
record that puts the round on a different day would make the wrong-door
conclusion look naive without erasing the finding that earned it.

## Events this draws on

Three entries for the event record, written as events with sources and stated
uncertainty, no explanation attached:

1. A transfer round ran on this street on this date. *(Sourced: counterfoil.)*
2. A named resident was recorded as no-contact and removed from the list.
   *(Sourced: desk copy. Unsigned — the annotation's author is unknown.)*
3. The house's supply was disconnected at the box before the grid failed.
   *(Sourced: physical state. Who did it is unknown.)*

None of the three says why. Several clues illuminate one event by design;
events are not issued one per clue.

## Open

- Which town the opening assumes, and whether the premise holds for a survivor
  who spawns somewhere without a numbered door.
- Whether the desk copy sits in a fixed kind of place or is selected per save.
- The reference code's shape, so it reads as furniture rather than a puzzle.
