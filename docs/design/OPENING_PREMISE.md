# The opening premise — "Recorded at another address"

Design document. Nothing here is built. The premise and evidence sequence for
the personal opening mystery (`DR-20260919-Q31`, and part B of
[the development plan](../management/DEVELOPMENT_TIMELINE_2026-09-19.md)).

Written payoff-first, per `DR-20260919-PAYOFF-SCOPE`: the conclusion is settled
below before a single clue is designed, and each clue exists to earn part of it.

**Revised after review.** The first draft over-claimed in three ways and
assumed a fourth thing it had no right to. All four are corrected below and the
corrections are the point of this version: the payoff is narrower, the visitor
is no longer part of it, completion is defined by a chain of findings rather
than a count, and nothing assumes the survivor owns the building they woke up
in.

---

## What the player ends up knowing

> **A collection was scheduled in my name. The record puts it at a different
> address from the one I am at. The record reports that visit as unsuccessful,
> and the entry cancelled.**

Read the wording carefully, because it is doing work. It says *the record
reports* — not that a party went to a door and found nobody. An unsigned
annotation establishes **what the record says**; it does not establish that the
visit happened as described. That distinction lives in the payoff itself, not
only in the checks.

**What it contributes to the wider search** — and deliberately does not settle:
whether the address was entered wrongly or altered, and whether the visit
described in the record took place at all. The case ends holding those, not
answering them. Nothing here explains Knox and nothing implies an explanation
exists (`DR-20260919-CONSISTENT-EVENTS`).

## The question this opening actually asks

Recorded explicitly rather than glossed. The agreed requirement names *"a
question about how or why the survivor arrived"*. **This premise does not answer
"why did I wake up here?" It answers "why was I left?"** — a deliberate
substitution, because the second question is the one that connects to the
central mystery and the first invites amnesia, which the survivor does not have.

If the opening must also address arrival, that is a different premise or an
additional one, and it should be chosen knowingly rather than assumed covered by
this one.

## The visitor is not part of the first payoff

The first draft claimed that someone with official access had been at the house.
Two observations were offered as equivalent roads to it, and **neither
establishes it**:

- A supply disconnected at the box does not identify who disconnected it, nor
  when relative to the grid failing, without further evidence.
- An undamaged door does not prove a key was used, that access was official, or
  that anyone visited at all.

Both are demoted to **optional observations** that colour the scene and
establish nothing. The visitor becomes a **separate, developing connection** —
if we want it established, it needs something attributable, such as a service
record naming a party and a date, with its claims kept distinct from what is
verified. That belongs to a later case, not this one.

## Completion: the essential chain

Completion is defined by **findings that must be established**, not by a count
of clues. Three links:

| | Must be established | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A collection was scheduled for the survivor | The retained slip |
| 2 | Its destination differs from the survivor's established location | Two verified world addresses, compared |
| 3 | A matching record reports an unsuccessful visit and cancellation | Either matching record below |

Where a link has two routes, **each route must support that same link on its
own**. The first draft failed this: it offered a counterfoil at the other
address as an alternative to the desk copy, but a completed counterfoil at
another address supports only *that a round ran on that street* — a different
claim entirely. It is corroboration, never a route to link 3.

### 1 — The retained slip *(placed by us, in reach at the start)*

A collection slip in the survivor's own name: a date, a reference code, an
address. Bureaucratic furniture — *"RETAIN THIS SLIP. Do not present it at the
assembly point."*

- **Observed:** a slip bearing your name, among belongings within reach.
- **Claims:** a collection was scheduled for that address on that date.
- **Supports:** a slip was issued in your name. Nothing about whether any
  collection happened.

### 2 — The address on the slip is not the address you are at

- **Observed:** the slip names one verified address; you are at another.
- **Supports:** the record's destination is not where you are.
- **Does not support:** that this was an error. An error and an alteration are
  indistinguishable from here, and the case must never claim to know which.

### 3 — A matching record *(either route establishes link 3)*

- **Route A — the desk copy.** A clipboard or carbon at a small official place,
  carrying the same reference struck through and annotated.
- **Route B — a returned notice.** The same reference on an undelivered notice
  sent back, carrying the same annotation.

For either: **Observed:** your reference, annotated. **Claims:** *"no contact at
premises"*, entry cancelled. **Supports:** a record reports the visit as
unsuccessful and the entry as cancelled. **Not** who wrote it, **not** why, and
**not** that the visit occurred as described.

### Optional, establishing nothing on its own

- A completed counterfoil at the slip's address — corroborates that a round ran
  on that street that day.
- *With electrical skill:* the supply was disconnected at the box. Who and when
  are unknown.
- *Without it:* the door shows no forcing. Proves no visit and no key.

The skill layer changes **how the survivor notices**, never whether the case can
finish, and never what is established. That is what makes this a pilot for the
remaining twenty-four occupations rather than a one-off.

## Addresses and locations: nothing assumed

The first draft assumed the spawn building was the survivor's home and that the
slip's address was the building next door. **Neither is true.**

- The address book identifies **buildings and numbers**. It does not establish
  **residence**, and this premise must not imply the survivor owns or lives in
  the building they woke up in.
- An adjacent building is **not** necessarily the next numbered address.
- Both addresses must be **verified world addresses** with **accessible
  carriers** — a container the player can actually reach, checked rather than
  assumed.
- **The placed slip is what makes this address matter.** It carries the
  survivor's name and ties them to a location without inventing home ownership.

## Recovery, and refusing to play

- **Interrupted or missed:** link 3 has two independent routes. Optional
  observations may be missed with no effect on completion.
- **Deliberate refusal:** no ending is forced. The slip stays in the organiser
  as an open lead (`DR-20260919-BADLY-PLAYED`).
- **Never:** a case that cannot reach an essential link must say so rather than
  quietly complete (`DR-20260919-SOLVABLE-WITHDRAWN`).

## What the follow-up inherits

The sourced finding — *a collection in my name was recorded at another address
and reported unsuccessful* — and two open questions: whether the address was
entered wrongly or altered, and whether the visit in the record happened at all.
The visitor thread starts here as a **question**, not a finding.

A follow-up may **challenge** the reading: a second record placing the round on
a different day would make the wrong-address conclusion look naive without
erasing the finding that earned it.

## Events this draws on

For the event record — events with sources and stated uncertainty, no
explanation attached:

1. A collection was scheduled against a named resident and a stated address.
   *(Sourced: the slip.)*
2. A record reports that collection unsuccessful and cancels the entry.
   *(Sourced: desk copy or returned notice. Unsigned — author unknown, and the
   report is not itself proof of the visit.)*
3. A collection round ran on that street on that date. *(Sourced: counterfoil.
   Corroboration only.)*

None says why. Several clues illuminate one event by design; events are not
issued one per clue.

## Open

- Which town the opening assumes, and whether the premise holds where the start
  has no numbered door nearby.
- Whether the matching record sits in a fixed kind of place or is selected per
  save, and how its carrier is verified reachable.
- How far apart the two addresses may be before the opening stops being local.
- The reference code's shape, so it reads as furniture rather than a puzzle.
- Whether an arrival question ("why did I wake up here?") is wanted as a
  separate opening, since this premise deliberately does not answer it.
