# Reference mystery: The collection that was cancelled

Complete authoring example for implementation, not a claim about the published build. Names illustrate the owner's screenshot. `{A}` and `{B}` are explicit binding slots, not text to show in game: A is the opening clue's actual location; B is a distinct verified destination with a readable address or identifiable landmark and directions. The renderer must resolve them before issuing a lead. No invented sample street number stands in for that requirement.

## Underlying event

An office accepted a named passenger-collection request using a borrowed **McCoy Logging Co.** vehicle. Before the scheduled run, its dispatcher reassigned the vehicle to fetch a replacement mill drive belt. A return entry recorded that the passenger run never departed. The retained passenger notice still tells the named person to wait.

McCoy is doing something specific to its business: keeping mill machinery working. The vanilla `McCoyLoggingCorp` flyer establishes the company, mill work, industrial equipment and truck-driving work; the installed `Base.VanJohnMcCoy` definition supplies its vehicle context. The one-off loan, booking, cancellation and people in this mystery are authored fiction. This does not establish a regular McCoy passenger service, make the survivor an employee, or turn A/B into its mill. B holds retained correspondence.

Q24's ongoing tone is **fatalistic, bureaucratic dark comedy**. The absurdity is that the vehicle, equipment and paperwork all complete their journeys while the passenger never starts. Official authors protect their priorities; the survivor notices what that costs them. The story should still be coherent with its jokes removed, and distinctly funny with them present. This remains an authored proposal awaiting reading/play feedback, not a claim that humour has been objectively verified.

The survivor's name appears on the booking. This establishes that the paperwork concerns them; it does not establish who booked it, that they agreed, that they waited, that A was their home, or that they have forgotten anything. Its transport purpose offers a concrete lead about an opportunity to leave, without calling it an evacuation or explaining Knox.

The local answer is: **this collection was cancelled before departure because the vehicle was reassigned.** The remaining personal question is: **who put my name on the request?** The story may establish what these records report and how they fit without treating every signature as infallible.

The documentary date is selected once before the game's starting date. Below, July 7–8 is the worked example for a July 9 start. Different start dates require a coherent historical calendar, not future-dated “old” records.

## Evidence 1: My collection notice

Carrier: a folded note in an appropriate correspondence container at A. This is the participant's retained copy, not an unexplained depot master ledger in a bedroom. Essential for the personal connection.

**Physical observation**

> A folded carbon slip. My name is typed above a box marked KEEP THIS COPY.

**On the document**

> HIRED COLLECTION — McCOY LOGGING CO. VEHICLE
>
> Booking PS-229 / issued July 7, 1993
> Passenger: Buddy Schuster
> Collection point: {A}
> Scheduled collection: July 8, 07:00
> Enquiries and retained booking file: {B}
>
> A borrowed McCoy vehicle is assigned for this one collection. Wait at the collection point.
> Keep this copy if the vehicle does not arrive.

**My note, when this is the only evidence known**

> That's my name. Someone booked a lift for me from {A}, in a borrowed McCoy vehicle. There's a file copy at {B}; I want to know whether they ever sent the driver. Useful of them to provide instructions for being left behind.

This neither asserts a remembered booking nor tells the player that an unseen second clue exists. The next place comes from the document itself.

## Evidence 2: The dispatcher changed the run

Carrier: a dated note retained with dispatch correspondence at B. Essential for the reason, but it can be discovered before the booking slip.

**Physical observation**

> A message clipped to a route card. “Passenger collection” is crossed out once, with the amendment signed underneath.

**On the document**

> July 8, 1993 — 05:40
> To dispatch / PS-229
>
> Use the borrowed McCoy vehicle assigned to the 07:00 passenger collection for the replacement mill drive belt instead. Do not send a driver to {A}.
>
> Keep the passenger request open until replacement transport is arranged.
> Do not mark the passenger absent; dispatch is the part not attending.
> Amendment authorised: Ines Kubiak.

**My note, alone**

> Ines wants the vehicle sent for a mill belt instead of a passenger. Keep the request open, send nobody. The passenger can continue waiting at no additional cost.

**My note, with evidence 1**

> PS-229. That's my booking. Ines told dispatch not to send a driver to the address on my slip. They were supposed to arrange another vehicle.

The document establishes an instruction. It does not yet establish that dispatch followed it.

## Evidence 3: The run never left

Carrier: a return-book page at B, separate from the amendment. Essential for the local result.

**Physical observation**

> A ruled dispatch page. The passenger and equipment entries occupy separate lines under the same vehicle number.

**On the document**

> DISPATCH RETURNS — July 8, 1993
>
> 07:00 passenger collection, PS-229, {A}: NOT DISPATCHED.
> Reason: vehicle reassigned under I. Kubiak amendment, 05:40.
> Replacement vehicle: none allocated.
>
> Mill drive-belt transfer: vehicle departed 07:10; returned 09:25.
> Passenger request PS-229: closed at end of shift. No collection recorded.
> Request copied from a call sheet signed “Ellis Hale”; caller's name not recorded. Enquiry copies retained at {B}.
>
> Vehicle log complete. Outstanding passenger requests: nil.

**My note, alone**

> The belt travelled. The passenger didn't. They closed the request without sending another vehicle, which brought the outstanding total down nicely. The original call sheet bears Ellis Hale's name; there's an enquiry copy at {B}.

**My note, with evidence 1**

> The run with my name on it never left, according to this return book. They used the vehicle for equipment and never allocated a replacement. My notice still says to wait.

**My note, with evidence 2**

> The return book cites Ines's amendment and records the equipment run. Her instruction kept the passenger request open; dispatch closed it without arranging another vehicle.

**Local conclusion, with all three**

> Now I have the notice, the order and the result: Ines reassigned the borrowed McCoy vehicle, and dispatch used it for the mill belt. The belt got a ride. I got instructions. That explains the missed collection; it doesn't explain who booked me onto it.

This is a bounded answer, not a claim that it explains the outbreak, proves deliberate abandonment, or establishes whether anyone survives now.

## Optional evidence with a purpose

A clerk's retained amendment copy can establish when the cancellation reached the counter. A payment receipt can establish that the booking deposit was refunded or retained; it must never be labelled contradictory merely because it predates the run. A physical key is eligible only if its tag/custody identifies a relevant object or it can be tested against an actual supported lock. Generic keys, arbitrary quantities of unrelated supplies and unconnected relay memos cannot pad this scenario.

Optional evidence may add a fact or sharpen a question. It must not be required for the three-source local conclusion. Nothing here requires a fixed clue count for every other mystery.

## Discovery and missing-evidence behavior

| Known evidence | What may be said |
|---|---|
| 1 | My name appears on a scheduled collection; B holds the booking file |
| 2 | A dispatcher ordered reassignment; whether it happened remains open |
| 3 | A return book records no passenger run, an equipment run, and no replacement |
| 1 + 2 | The reassignment instruction concerns my booking |
| 1 + 3 | The return book records that my booked run never departed |
| 2 + 3 | The return book cites the amendment; closure differs from its instruction to retain the request |
| 1 + 2 + 3 | Personal connection, reason, recorded result and unsupported remaining question are distinct |

Every discovery order converges on the same source facts. Later discoveries add comparisons without rewriting the original document. If 1 is missing, there is no personal conclusion. If 2 is missing, the dispatch account is known but its separate authorisation has not been found. If 3 is missing, an order is not silently promoted into an executed journey. Missing evidence does not count as a completed investigation.

## Coherent variation

This cancellation variant is one event, not one half of a mandatory innocent/sinister switch. A different variant may document a vehicle arriving at the wrong collection point, but then the notice, amendment, return entry, participant reactions and local answer all change together. Names, reference and dates vary within roles. No variation changes the survivor's biography or turns a player's theory into a historical fact.

Do not introduce a compulsory follow-up to compensate for a missing ending. This case already answers what happened to this booking. A later case can investigate who submitted it only when an actual discovered source gives a usable lead. Otherwise retain that personal question honestly as open.
